Chapter 1, "Les enjeux de la production de connaissances" (The challenges of knowledge production), briefly covers the concept of modernity, global extinctions and threats, the nuclear winter hypothesis, and climate change and CO2 emissions.

It establishes that modern society, rooted in the Protestant Reformation and the Enlightenment, places trust in reason for progress and rational organization for economic and social advancement. Science plays a crucial role in intellectual autonomy, informing political decisions, and driving innovation.

This chapter highlights the urgent need to distinguish scientific knowledge from mere beliefs, especially in the face of global threats like climate change and nuclear conflict. It also discusses the acceleration of species extinction, noting humanity's significant impact, and introduces the "Doomsday Clock" as a conceptual measure of global catastrophe risk.

The chapter emphasizes the importance of science in assessing the consequences of political decisions, acknowledging the challenges of integrating scientific uncertainties into governance and the potential for science to be instrumentalized.
